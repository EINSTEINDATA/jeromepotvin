Your AI drives a Ferrari. Who pays for the gas?

Jerome (Jay) Potvin, Director, Commercial Strategy, Innovair Solutions
October 25, 2026

We budget AI like software. Yet it is paid by usage, in tokens. A license is paid once per user. A token is paid with every question, every answer, every step an agent takes. The more successful the project, the faster the meter runs.

Source: KPMG, AI Pulse Q2 2026, via Enterprise DNA.

The price per gallon: the token

A token is a piece of text. A thousand tokens represent about 750 words in English. Providers bill per million tokens, with two prices: what you send to the model, and what it sends back. The answer costs 5 times more.

The same million tokens can cost up to 100 times more depending on the model

What keeps the meter running

The price per million seems trivial. What drives up the bill is the number of tokens actually consumed per task. It is almost always underestimated.

In the field, people drive without a license

I spoke with electrical distributors and industrial manufacturers. Most manage AI with an overall budget, with no clear idea of what tokens cost depending on the model. All are in test mode and plan to adjust course when the invoices arrive.

Giving access to AI without rules or cost tracking is like handing the keys to a Ferrari to someone without a driver’s license.

Surveys by Deloitte, KPMG, Flexera and PagerDuty published in 2026 describe the same situation, including in large companies. SMBs have every reason to address it now.

The car that stays in the garage

The project pays for itself with the time saved: 12 minutes per quote. But the build, platform, maintenance and governance cost the same whether the agent is used 20,000 or 5,000 times. Drag the slider.

At 100%, the project is profitable in the first year. At 25%, it leaves a loss of about $74,000 after three years. Same agent, same quote: only usage changed.

A word of caution: time saved only becomes money if it is used for something else. More quotes, more customer follow-up, less overtime. Otherwise, it is a comfort gain.

Who pays to fill the tank?

A license has a clear owner: IT. A token bill, on the other hand, originates in sales, customer service or purchasing, but often lands in the IT budget. Result: nobody is watching, or we slow down what works best.

The answer looks like what we already do for freight: tie the cost to the activity that generates it. Each use case has a business owner, a cost per task tracked monthly, and a threshold that triggers a review rather than a cut.

The token deserves the same rigor as a gross margin.

Before you approve your next AI project, know what one task costs, how many tasks per year, what the same task costs when done by a person, and what the full project costs over three years.

And you: do you know the cost per task of your AI projects?
